Search and Rescue GPS Tracking Without Cell Service

Short answer: Loko shows where your own team, search dogs and drones are, within radio range of a Loko Ground you carry, with no SIM, no plan and no mobile network. It is not a distress beacon and cannot call for help.

Range is 20+ km in open areas with line of sight, and much less among trees, hills and buildings. This page sets out the tracking jobs in a search, which tools fit each, and plainly where Loko fits and where it doesn't.

Updated 18 November 2026. Garmin prices checked on Garmin's website on 13 November 2026 and ACR prices on ACR's website on 13 November 2026. Prices change, so check again before you buy.

Loko is not an emergency device. It has no SOS button and no messaging. For distress alerting, every team should carry a personal locator beacon or a satellite communicator.

The Jobs

Three Different Tracking Jobs in a Search

  • Team map: seeing where your searchers, dogs and drones are during the operation.
  • Distress alerting: calling outside help when someone, including a searcher, is in trouble.
  • Debrief track: looking back at where each team actually went, to see what ground was covered.

No single device does all three well. Most teams combine tools.

Where Loko Fits

Where Loko Helps a SAR Team

  • Team accountability: a 14 g Loko Air on each searcher, and one Ground at the command post following up to 30 trackers.
  • Search dogs: a Loko Air on the dog shows the handler and command where it is ranging.
  • Drones: a Loko Air on a drone helps find it if it comes down.
  • Training: running exercises where there is no coverage, with no plan to pay for.
  • A located subject during carry-out: keeping the evacuation party on the team map.

Range improves with height: a Ground on a ridge or vehicle roof usually hears further than one held low in a valley. That is general radio behaviour, so test it in your own terrain.

Where Loko Doesn't Fit

When Loko Is the Wrong Tool

  • Distress alerting: no SOS, no messaging, and no relay or mesh.
  • Beyond radio range: a searcher out of range of every Ground drops off the map.
  • Underground: it needs a satellite fix and a radio path to the Ground, so it is not for caves.
  • Water: Loko is not waterproof; light rain only.
  • Long operations at fast updates: short intervals drain the battery much faster.
  • Feeding an agency mapping system: plan to check how positions reach your existing tools before you rely on it.
  • Certified equipment: Loko holds no certifications, so check your local radio rules for the frequency you set (see the LoRa frequency guide).

For operating temperature and other hardware details, see the specifications.

Reading the Map

What the Map Shows When a Searcher Drops Out

The app keeps the last position it received with the time it arrived, but shows no warning that it is old, so always read the time before acting on a dot. If a tracker cannot get a satellite fix within 60 seconds, it sends its last known position. Out of range, the Loko Air keeps logging up to 3,100 positions on the device, which you can read later over USB with the configurator for a debrief. The Ground stores positions while the phone is disconnected, but the app does not pull them in when you reconnect.

Battery

Battery on an Operation

Battery life depends on the update interval: up to 1 year at long intervals, and much less at the short intervals a search usually needs. No runtime at search intervals is published yet, so test the interval you plan to use before an operation, and start each operation charged. The Ground runs on an 18650 battery, which is not included.

Checklist

Before an Operation

  1. Give every searcher a beacon or satellite communicator for distress alerting.
  2. Set the same frequency on every Loko Air and the Ground, within your local rules.
  3. Charge every tracker and fit a charged 18650 in the Ground.
  4. Choose the update interval and check the battery holds for the operational period.
  5. Pick a high spot for the Ground and test range across the search area.
  6. Agree how the team treats a position whose time is old.

FAQ

Questions SAR Teams Ask

Can Loko call for help in an emergency?

No. Loko has no SOS and no messaging and cannot contact rescue services. For distress alerting, carry a personal locator beacon or a satellite communicator.

How far does Loko reach in search terrain?

Loko works within radio range of your Loko Ground: 20+ km in open areas with line of sight, and much less among trees, hills and buildings. Plan receiver positions with that in mind.

How many searchers can one Loko Ground follow?

Up to 30 Loko Air trackers per Ground. A kit is $119 for one Air and one Ground, and each extra Air is $59.

Does Loko need a subscription or a network?

No. The Loko Air sends its position over LoRa radio straight to the Loko Ground, which passes it to the app on a phone by Bluetooth. There is no SIM, no plan and no mobile network involved.

What does the map show when a searcher goes out of range?

The app keeps the last position it received with the time it arrived, but shows no warning that it is old, so always check the time. The tracker keeps logging positions on the device, which you can read later over USB.

How long does the battery last on a search?

It depends on the update interval: up to 1 year at long intervals, and much less at the short intervals a search usually needs. Test your interval before an operation and carry spare power.
