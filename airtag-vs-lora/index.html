AirTag Range vs a LoRa GPS Tracker

Three Kinds of AirTag Distance, and Where a Local-Radio Tracker Fits

"How far does an AirTag reach?" has three answers, because an AirTag can be found in three different ways: directly over Bluetooth from your own iPhone, with Precision Finding, and through Apple's Find My network. Each works over a different distance, and Apple gives no figure in metres for any of them.

This page explains the three distances, gives the figures from two independent tests, shows where an AirTag stops being useful, and explains where a local-radio tracker like Loko fits. It also says plainly where Loko is the wrong choice.

The Three Kinds of AirTag Distance

An AirTag has no GPS. It sends out a Bluetooth signal, and an Apple device that picks it up works out where the AirTag is. The three ways of finding it differ in which device hears it and how close that device has to be.

  1. Direct Bluetooth from your own iPhone. When your iPhone is close enough to hear the AirTag, Find My can play its sound and show it as nearby. Apple says the second-generation AirTag has an upgraded Bluetooth chip that "expands the range", but gives no distance.
  2. Precision Finding. At closer range, Ultra Wideband in a compatible iPhone shows an arrow and a distance to the AirTag. Apple says the second-generation AirTag can be found this way from "up to 50 percent farther away than the previous generation". Again, there is no metre figure. The expanded range needs an iPhone Air or iPhone 15 or later (not iPhone 16e), and Apple says availability varies by region.
  3. The Find My network. When you are out of range, any nearby iPhone, iPad or Mac in the Find My network that hears the AirTag sends its location to iCloud, anonymously and encrypted, and you see it in the Find My app. This has no fixed range: it covers wherever Apple devices happen to pass close to the AirTag, and nowhere else.

Japan: on its Japanese newsroom page, Apple states that because of regulations the expanded Precision Finding range is not available in Japan, and that Precision Finding on Apple Watch cannot be used there.

Because Apple publishes no distances, the only figures come from testers. These are single tests with one set of devices and conditions. Treat them as examples, not guarantees.

Where an AirTag Fails

  • Where no Apple devices pass by. Out of your own range, an AirTag's location only updates when someone else's Apple device comes close enough to hear it. On a remote trail, a farm or in a forest, that can mean no new location for a long time. Find My then shows the last place a device heard it, which may be far from where the item is now.
  • Without an iPhone or iPad. Setting up and finding an AirTag needs an Apple account and an iPhone or iPad. Android phones can alert you to an unknown AirTag travelling with you, but they cannot find yours.
  • Behind walls and bodies. In Macwelt's test, range dropped from 110 m in the open to 14 m or less with the AirTag in a back pocket.
  • Precision Finding in Japan. The expanded range is not available there.

Android has its own crowdsourced system. In April 2024 Google launched its Find My Device network, built on "over a billion Android devices", with Bluetooth tags from Chipolo and Pebblebee. AirTag does not use it, and as a crowdsourced network it also relies on phones passing near the tag.

Where a Local-Radio Tracker Like Loko Fits

Loko works without anyone else's phone. You carry the receiver yourself:

  1. The Loko Air tracker (14 g, 30 × 22 mm) gets its own position from GPS satellites.
  2. It sends that position over LoRa radio, peer to peer, to the Loko Ground receiver you carry.
  3. Loko Ground passes it to the Loko app on your phone over Bluetooth.

There is no SIM card, no subscription and no network of other people's devices. The range between tracker and receiver is 20+ km in open areas with a clear line of sight. Trees, hills and buildings shorten it, so test it where you plan to use it. One Loko Ground can follow up to 30 trackers.

Two things to know before you rely on it:

  • If the tracker cannot get a satellite fix within 60 seconds, it sends its last known position. The app shows the time each position arrived but no warning that it is old, so check the time before you walk to a point.
  • The tracker keeps a log of up to 3,100 positions, so the route it took while out of range is not lost.

Where Loko Is the Wrong Choice

  • Keys, bags and luggage in towns and airports. Here the Find My network is everywhere, you carry nothing extra, and an AirTag costs $29.
  • Anything that gets wet. Loko is not waterproof and handles light rain only. An AirTag is rated IP67.
  • Things that may end up far beyond your receiver. Loko only reports to a Loko Ground within radio range. If the tracker is stolen and driven away, there is no network to pick it up.
  • Fast updates for a long time. The "up to 1 year" battery figure applies to long update intervals. At short intervals, the battery runs down much faster.

An AirTag suits you when:

  • You use an iPhone
  • The item stays where people with Apple devices pass by
  • You want the cheapest tag and nothing extra to carry
  • The item might get wet

Loko suits you when:

  • You are where few or no phones pass by
  • You want positions from the tracker's own GPS, sent straight to you
  • You use Android, or a mix of phones
  • You are following pets, livestock or drones outdoors and can carry the receiver

AirTag vs LoRa GPS Tracker: FAQ

What is the range of an AirTag?

There are three ranges, and Apple gives no figure in metres for any of them. Direct Bluetooth from your own iPhone and Precision Finding only work close by; in a single test by Stiftung Warentest (March 2026), the second-generation AirTag reached about 60 m over Bluetooth outdoors and about 25 m with Precision Finding. The Find My network has no fixed range: it works wherever another Apple device passes close enough to hear the AirTag.

Does AirTag have GPS?

No. An AirTag sends a Bluetooth signal. A nearby Apple device that hears it sends its own location to iCloud, and that is what you see in Find My.

Can an AirTag work in remote areas or forests?

Only within range of your own iPhone. Beyond that, its location updates only when another Apple device passes close by, which may not happen in remote areas. Find My then shows the last place a device heard it.

Is AirTag Precision Finding available in Japan?

Apple states that because of regulations the expanded Precision Finding range of the second-generation AirTag is not available in Japan, and that Precision Finding on Apple Watch cannot be used there.

What is the range of Loko?

20+ km in open areas with a clear line of sight between the Loko Air tracker and the Loko Ground receiver. Trees, hills and buildings shorten it. Loko does not use other people's phones, so it only reports to a receiver within radio range.

Which is better for pets, luggage or outdoor gear?

For luggage and everyday items in towns, an AirTag is cheaper and the Find My network covers you. For pets, livestock or gear in places where few phones pass by, Loko sends its own GPS position straight to the receiver you carry. Loko is not waterproof, so it does not suit anything that goes in water.

An AirTag is found by the Apple devices around it. Loko sends its own GPS position to a receiver you carry, with no SIM and no subscription.

Loko costs $119 for a tracker and receiver.
