How They Work — The Fundamental Difference

Tractive: Cellular-Based Tracking

Tractive connects to its servers through a cellular network (LTE-M or NB-IoT). When you check your dog's location, the tag sends its position over the mobile network to Tractive's cloud server, which then pushes that data to your phone app.

This works brilliantly within range of cell towers. But when you head into forests, mountains, or rural areas without coverage, Tractive cannot send any data. It simply stops reporting until it regains signal.

And there's the cost: every Tractive tag requires an active SIM and a monthly subscription plan — typically $5–$15 per month per tag, depending on the region and plan tier.

Loko: LoRa Radio + Real GPS

Loko receives coordinates directly from GPS satellites (GPS, GLONASS, Galileo) — just like a smartphone's GPS module. It then transmits those coordinates over LoRa radio — a long-range low-power wireless technology — directly to your handheld Loko Ground receiver.

The entire chain involves zero internet, zero cellular networks, zero servers: GPS satellites → Loko Air → LoRa radio (~20 km) → Loko Ground → Bluetooth → Your Phone.

No one owns your data. No company can go offline and break your connection. And no monthly bill comes due at the end of each month.

Battery Life

This is arguably the most dramatic difference between the two products.

  • Tractive: Lasts 1–5 days depending on update frequency and signal strength. In areas with poor signal, the tag has to transmit harder, draining battery even faster.
  • Loko: Lasts up to 1 year on a single charge. Because LoRa radio consumes milliwatts of power during transmission (versus tens or hundreds of milliwatts for cellular), and updates are sent only when you actively query a position, battery life extends dramatically.

If you want to deploy a tracker across a large property, attach it to livestock that roams freely, or track animals during multi-day hunting trips without worrying about recharging — Loko's battery advantage is decisive.

When Each Tracker Makes Sense

Choose Tractive if:

  • You primarily need to track a pet in an urban or suburban area
  • Your phone carrier coverage is excellent and consistent everywhere your pet goes
  • You want someone else to manage the SIM card and cellular billing for you
  • You don't mind paying $5–$15/month indefinitely

Choose Loko if:

  • You hunt, farm, hike, or spend time in areas without cell service
  • You want real GPS coordinates (not crowd-sourced Bluetooth triangulation)
  • You prefer one-time payment over recurring subscriptions
  • You need battery life measured in months or years, not days
  • You want to track multiple animals/assets simultaneously from one receiver

FAQ

Is Loko better than Tractive?

It depends on your use case. For remote areas, hunting, farming, or anywhere without reliable mobile coverage, Loko is significantly better. For urban pet tracking with constant cell service, both work — but Loko saves you ongoing costs.

What are the main differences between Loko and Tractive?

Tractive uses cellular networks (requires SIM + monthly fees). Loko uses LoRa radio (no SIM, no fees, up to 20 km range). Loko also has real GPS built in; Tractive relies on crowdsourced nearby device detection via Bluetooth.

Can I replace Tractive with Loko?

If you primarily use Tractive in cities or suburban areas with good cell coverage, Loko can replace it if you carry the handheld receiver within 20 km of your tracked item. For truly remote locations where Tractive stops working anyway, Loko is more reliable.

Does Loko work internationally?

Yes. Loko operates on licence-free ISM bands (868 MHz in Europe/UK, 915 MHz in USA/Australia) and uses standard GPS/GLONASS/Galileo/BeiDou satellites. It works anywhere on Earth with clear line-of-sight — no roaming charges.
