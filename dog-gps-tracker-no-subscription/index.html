Best Dog Tracker Without a Subscription: 4 Ways to Skip the Monthly Fee

Bluetooth tags, cellular trackers with data included, radio collars and LoRa trackers compared, with prices from the makers and a 3-year cost for 1 and 3 dogs.

Most GPS dog trackers charge a monthly fee because they send the dog's position over the mobile network. There are four ways to avoid that fee, and each one gives something up: GPS, coverage, weight or price.

This page compares them using the makers' own prices and specs, and says plainly where Loko fits and where it doesn't.

Prices checked on the makers' websites on 5 November 2026. They change, so check again before you buy.

Four Ways to Track a Dog Without a Monthly Fee

  1. Bluetooth tag (for example Apple AirTag). No GPS. Nearby Apple devices report where they saw the tag. Cheap and light, but weak where there are no people.
  2. Cellular tracker with data included (for example PitPat). Real GPS sent over the mobile network, with the maker paying for the data. Needs mobile coverage where the dog is.
  3. Radio collar with a handheld or phone app (for example Aorkuler, Dogtra PATHFINDER2, Garmin Alpha). GPS on the collar, sent by radio to a receiver you carry. Works without coverage, within radio range.
  4. LoRa tracker (Loko). Also GPS by radio to a receiver you carry, in a 14 g tracker that shows the dog in a phone app.

Where Loko Fits

  • Rural homes, farms, training fields, hunting areas and hikes where you stay within radio range.
  • Places with weak or no mobile coverage.
  • Several dogs: one Loko Ground follows up to 30 trackers, and each extra tracker is $59 with no per-dog plan.
  • Small dogs, where a 14 g tracker matters.

Where Loko Doesn't Fit

  • Dogs that swim. Loko is not waterproof and handles light rain only.
  • Dogs that might end up far away. If your dog could run into the next town, you need a cellular tracker. Loko only reports within radio range of your receiver.
  • City dogs. In busy areas a Bluetooth tag or cellular tracker may be enough.
  • Training. Loko has no stimulation, tone or vibration.

Know these limits too: the Loko Ground runs on an 18650 battery that is not included. If the tracker cannot get a satellite fix within 60 seconds, it sends its last known position, and the app shows when each position arrived but no warning that it is old. We have not yet published a measured battery runtime at the short intervals used for following a dog.

FAQ

What is the best dog tracker without a subscription?

It depends on where your dog goes. In towns, a Bluetooth tag may be enough. With good mobile coverage, a cellular tracker with data included works. In remote areas, a radio tracker such as a hunting collar or Loko is the better fit.

Does Loko charge a subscription per dog?

No. Each extra Loko Air tracker is a one-time $59, and one Loko Ground follows up to 30 trackers.

How far can I track my dog with Loko?

20+ km in open areas with a clear line of sight. Trees, hills and buildings shorten the range.

Is an AirTag a GPS tracker?

No. AirTag has no GPS chip. It is found when nearby Apple devices pick up its Bluetooth signal, so it works poorly where there are few people.

Is Loko waterproof?

No. Loko handles light rain only, so it is not suitable for dogs that swim.
