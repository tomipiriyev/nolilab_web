Why Dog Trackers Usually Have Monthly Fees

Dog trackers need a way to send the dog's location back to you. Many products use LTE or LTE-M, which requires a carrier plan. That is why the subscription is usually tied to the device.

For one dog this may be manageable. For multiple dogs, hunting packs, or farm dogs, per-device subscription costs become expensive quickly.

Where Loko Fits for Dogs

Loko is best for local tracking: farms, rural homes, hunting areas, training fields, campsites, and hikes where the handler remains within radio range.

It is also useful when you do not want your pet's location routed through a third-party cloud service for everyday outdoor tracking.

When to Choose Another Dog Tracker

If you need to track a dog from another city or receive location updates when nobody nearby has the receiver, a cellular dog tracker is the correct category.

If your dog is mostly in dense city areas, AirTag-style crowd tracking may be enough. For remote outdoor use, direct GPS plus LoRa is a better match.

Dog Tracker Without Subscription: How It Works

Finding a dog tracker without a subscription means looking beyond cellular-based devices. Traditional GPS dog trackers pair a GPS receiver with an LTE modem — the GPS finds the location, and the modem sends it to a cloud server over the cellular network. That modem is why you pay a monthly fee.

Loko removes the cellular layer entirely. The GPS receiver calculates coordinates, and a LoRa radio transmits them directly to your handheld receiver. No cloud, no SIM, no subscription. The entire tracking chain is local radio, from satellite to your phone.

This makes Loko ideal for hunting dogs, farm dogs, and pets in rural or off-grid areas where cell coverage is unreliable or expensive.
