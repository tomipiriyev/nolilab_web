GPS Tracker Without Cell Service

Seven ways a tracker's position can reach you when there are no towers, and how to pick one.

Every GPS tracker can work out its position without cell service. The question is how that position gets to you. Most trackers use the mobile network, so they stop updating when coverage ends.

There are seven other routes. The right one depends mostly on one question: will you be near the tracker?

Will You Be Near It?

  • Yes, within a few kilometres: local radio is the simplest and has no running cost.
  • Sometimes, and you can install equipment: LoRaWAN gateways can cover a farm or site.
  • No, it could be anywhere: satellite is the only route that works without towers or receivers nearby, and it usually needs a plan.
  • You only need the track afterwards: a logger is enough.

How Loko Works Without Cell Service

Loko Air works out its position from GPS, GLONASS and Galileo satellites and sends it by LoRa radio straight to your Loko Ground, which passes it to the Loko app over Bluetooth. No SIM, no server and no subscription. Loko can also run in LoRaWAN mode; see the LoRaWAN guide for The Things Network.

Know Loko's Limits

  • You must be within radio range of your Loko Ground: 20+ km in open areas with line of sight, much less among trees, hills and buildings.
  • If the tracker cannot get a satellite fix within 60 seconds, it sends its last known position. The app shows when each position arrived but no warning that it is old, so check the time.
  • Out of range, Loko Air keeps logging up to 3,100 positions on the device (oldest overwritten first). The Ground stores positions while your phone is disconnected, but the app does not pull them in when you reconnect.
  • Loko is not waterproof; it withstands light rain only. The Ground runs on an 18650 battery, which is not included.
  • Battery life depends on the update interval: up to 1 year at long intervals, much less at short ones.

When You Need Satellite Instead

If the tracker could end up far from anyone carrying a receiver, a satellite tracker or messenger is the right category. Loko is for local tracking, where you are in the same broad area as the tracker.

FAQ

Can a GPS tracker work without cell service?

Yes. GPS needs no cell service. The tracker also needs another way to send the position: a logger, local radio, LoRaWAN or satellite.

Does Loko need a SIM card?

No. Loko sends positions by LoRa radio to your own receiver, so there is no SIM or monthly plan.

Is Loko a satellite tracker?

No. Loko uses satellites to find its position, but sends it by radio to a receiver near you. For tracking from anywhere, use a satellite product.

What happens when Loko is out of range?

The tracker keeps logging positions on the device, but you will not see them live until you are back in range.

Loko sends GPS positions by radio to a receiver you carry: no towers, no SIM, no subscription.
