GPS Tracker With No Monthly Fee: What You Really Pay Over 5 Years

“No monthly fee” happens in four ways, and each one trades the fee for a limit.

A tracker works out its position from satellites for free. The fee pays for getting that position to you, usually over a mobile network. A tracker can skip the monthly fee in four ways: borrowing other people's phones, prepaying the network, using your own SIM and server, or sending straight to a receiver you carry. This page compares what each costs over five years, using makers' own prices, and says plainly where Loko fits and where it doesn't.

Updated 19 November 2026. Tractive prices checked on Tractive's website on that date; Garmin prices checked on 13 November 2026. Prices change, so check again before you buy.

Before You Buy a “Lifetime SIM” Tracker

  1. Which network does it use? Older networks are being switched off in many countries; a tracker on a closing network stops working.
  2. Whose lifetime? Usually the device's or the service's, not yours. Read what happens if the maker closes or ends the service.
  3. Where does it have coverage? Prepaid or not, it needs mobile coverage wherever the tracker goes.

Choose in Six Questions

  1. Will you be near the tracker, or need to see it from anywhere?
  2. Is there mobile coverage where it goes?
  3. Do you need SOS or messaging?
  4. How many things are you tracking?
  5. Will the tracker get wet?
  6. How often do you need a new position?

Near the tracker, often without coverage, several dogs, drones or pieces of gear, dry conditions: a local radio tracker like Loko fits. Far away, or needing SOS: pick a cellular or satellite tracker and budget for the plan.

How Loko Works With No Fee

The 14 g Loko Air gets its position from GPS and sends it over LoRa radio to a Loko Ground you keep with you. The Ground passes it to the Loko app on your phone by Bluetooth. No SIM, no mobile network and no plan are involved, and one Ground can follow up to 30 trackers. You set the radio frequency yourself; see the LoRa frequency guide.

Know Loko's Limits

  • Range: 20+ km in open areas with line of sight, much less among trees, hills and buildings.
  • Out of range, the app keeps the last position with the time it arrived, but shows no warning that it is old. The Air keeps logging up to 3,100 positions, which you can read later over USB.
  • Battery life depends on the update interval: up to 1 year at long intervals, much less at short ones.
  • No SOS, no messaging, and no relay or mesh.
  • Not waterproof; light rain only.
  • Loko holds no certifications, so check your local radio rules for the frequency you set.

When Loko Is the Wrong Choice

  • A parked car or trailer you want to watch from home: it will be far outside radio range. Use a cellular tracker.
  • Emergencies: Loko cannot call for help. Use a beacon or satellite communicator.
  • Water: Loko is not waterproof.

FAQ

Can a GPS tracker really have no monthly fee?

Yes, but each way of doing it has a limit. Bluetooth tags rely on nearby phones, prepaid “lifetime” SIM trackers depend on the maker and the network, and local radio trackers like Loko only work within range of your own receiver.

Does Loko have any subscription or SIM?

No. The Loko Air sends its position over LoRa radio straight to the Loko Ground, and the Ground passes it to the app on your phone by Bluetooth. There is no SIM, no plan and no mobile network.

What does Loko cost over 5 years?

$119 for one Air and one Ground, plus an 18650 battery for the Ground, which is not included. Each extra Air is $59. There is no plan to pay after that.

How far does Loko reach?

Within radio range of your Loko Ground: 20+ km in open areas with line of sight, and much less among trees, hills and buildings. Beyond that range you see the last position received.

When is a no-fee tracker the wrong choice?

When you need to see the tracker from anywhere, such as a parked car across town, or when you need SOS. For those jobs a cellular or satellite tracker with a plan is the better fit.
