GPS Tracker for Hiking Without Cell Service

Three different jobs, and the tools that actually do each one

"GPS tracker for hiking" can mean three different things, and no single device does all three well:

1. Finding your own way. Your phone's GPS works without signal if you download maps before you leave. 2. Getting help in an emergency. That needs a personal locator beacon (PLB) or a satellite messenger. 3. Seeing where a nearby partner, dog or piece of gear is. That is what a local radio tracker such as Loko does.

Loko is not an emergency device. It has no SOS button and no messaging, and it cannot contact rescue services. If you might need to call for help where there is no cell service, carry a PLB or a satellite messenger as well.

Where Loko Fits on a Hike

Loko is two parts. The Loko Air (14 g, 30 × 22 mm) goes with the person, dog or gear you want to keep track of. It sends its position over LoRa radio to the Loko Ground you carry, and the Ground passes it to the app on your phone by Bluetooth. No SIM, no subscription, and one Ground can follow up to 30 trackers.

  • A partner who walks ahead or takes a side trail: you see where they are while they stay in radio range.
  • A pack or gear left at a junction or camp: you can find it again on the way back.
  • A group spread out on a route: one Ground can show several trackers at once.
  • Two people following the same tracker: more than one Ground can receive the same Air if each Ground is set to the same frequency and ID.

Range is 20+ km in open areas with line of sight, and much less among trees, hills and ridges. Changing the update interval does not change the range. The Loko Air only sends its position; it has no messaging, so the person carrying it cannot reply or ask for help through Loko.

Where Loko Is the Wrong Choice

  • You might need to call for help: carry a PLB or a satellite messenger. Loko has no SOS.
  • Family at home want to follow you: Loko does not send anything to the internet, so only people with a Loko Ground nearby can see the location. A satellite messenger can share it.
  • You want to send or receive messages: Loko has no messaging. Meshtastic devices or a satellite messenger can.
  • You need a relay over a ridge: Loko has no relay or mesh; each Air talks straight to the Ground.
  • Wet conditions or river crossings: Loko is not waterproof and withstands light rain only.

Five Questions Before You Choose

  1. Do you need to call for help where there is no signal? If yes, start with a PLB or a satellite messenger.
  2. Who needs to see the location: you on the trail, or someone at home? Someone at home means a satellite service.
  3. Do you need to send messages? If yes, look at a satellite messenger or Meshtastic.
  4. Is the person, dog or gear you track usually within a few kilometres of you? If yes, a local radio tracker such as Loko works without fees.
  5. Will you pay a monthly plan? Satellite messengers need one; PLBs, Meshtastic and Loko do not.

What the App Shows When a Tracker Goes Out of Range

The app keeps the last position it received and shows the time it arrived, but no warning that the position is old, so always check the time. If the tracker cannot get a satellite fix within 60 seconds, it sends its last known position.

Out of range, the Loko Air keeps logging up to 3,100 positions on the device, with the oldest overwritten first. The Ground stores positions while your phone is disconnected, but the app does not pull them in when you reconnect.

Battery on Multi-Day Trips

Battery life depends on the update interval: up to 1 year at long intervals, much less at short ones. We have not yet published a measured runtime at the short intervals hikers usually choose, so test your setting before a long route and plan to charge between trips. The Ground runs on an 18650 battery, which is not included.

FAQ

Do I need cell service for Loko to work on a hike?

No. Loko Air sends its position over LoRa radio straight to the Loko Ground, which passes it to the app by Bluetooth. There is no SIM and no subscription. You do need to stay within radio range of the Ground: 20+ km in open areas with line of sight, much less among trees, hills and ridges.

Can Loko call for help in an emergency?

No. Loko has no SOS button and no messaging, and it cannot reach rescue services. If you need to call for help without cell service, carry a personal locator beacon or a satellite messenger. Loko is for seeing where a partner, a dog or your gear is within radio range.

Who can see the location from a Loko Air?

Only people with a Loko Ground and the app, within radio range. More than one Ground can receive the same Air if each Ground is set to the same frequency and ID. Nobody at home can see it, because Loko does not send anything to the internet. If family at home need to follow you, you need a satellite messenger.
