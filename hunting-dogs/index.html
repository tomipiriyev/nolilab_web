GPS Tracker for Hunting Dogs Without Cell Service

Garmin Alpha 300, Dogtra PATHFINDER2, Dogtrace X30 and Loko compared

Hunting dogs often run where phones show no bars. A tracker that sends your dog's position over the mobile network stops updating there, so most hunters use a system with its own radio link between the collar and a receiver you carry.

This page compares four of those systems using the makers' own published specs, then gives six questions to help you choose. It also says plainly where Loko is the wrong choice.

Prices and specs checked on the makers' websites on 5 November 2026. They change, so check again before you buy.

How a Dog Tracker Works Without Cell Service

Each system below has two parts: a GPS unit on the dog, and a receiver with you. The collar works out its position from satellites and sends it to your receiver by radio. No SIM card, no tower and no internet connection sit between them.

What differs is the receiver. Garmin and Dogtrace use a dedicated handheld with its own screen. Dogtra's PATHFINDER2 and Loko show the dog on your phone, which talks to a small receiver over Bluetooth.

Range depends on terrain. Every maker quotes range for open ground with a clear line between the dog and you. Trees, hills and valleys shorten it, so treat the headline figure as a best case.

Six Questions Before You Choose

  1. Does your dog swim or retrieve from water? If yes, you need a waterproof collar. Garmin, Dogtra and Dogtrace all publish a water rating. Loko does not: it handles light rain only.
  2. Do you train with the collar? If you want stimulation, tone or vibration from the same collar, look at Dogtra PATHFINDER2 or the training versions from Garmin and Dogtrace. Loko only tracks.
  3. Handheld or phone? A dedicated handheld keeps working when your phone battery is flat. A phone-based system is lighter to carry and you already know the screen.
  4. What is your terrain? All four quote range in the open. In thick forest or deep valleys, expect much less. If you can, test the system in your own hunting ground before you rely on it.
  5. How big is your dog? The hunting collars above weigh well over 100 g. The Loko tracker weighs 14 g, which matters more on a small terrier or a young dog than on a large hound.
  6. What is your budget? None of the four needs a subscription, but the upfront cost ranges from $119 for a Loko kit to about $1,100 for a Garmin handheld and collar.

Where Loko Is the Wrong Choice

  • Water dogs. Loko is not waterproof. Do not use it on dogs that swim, retrieve from water or work in heavy rain.
  • Training. Loko has no stimulation, tone or vibration. It is a tracker, not an e-collar.
  • Long hunts with fast updates. The "up to 1 year" battery figure applies to long update intervals. At the short intervals hunters often use, the battery runs down much faster, and we have not yet published a measured runtime for those settings.
  • You want a dedicated handheld. Loko shows the dog on your phone. If your phone dies, you lose the map view.

Where Loko Fits

Loko suits hunters who want a light, low-cost tracker for dogs that stay out of the water, and who are happy to use their phone as the screen.

1

Loko Air (on the dog)

A 14 g GPS tracker, 30 × 22 mm, that sends its position over LoRa radio. It keeps a log of up to 3,100 positions, oldest overwritten first, and keeps logging when it is out of range of the receiver.

2

Loko Ground (with you)

Receives the positions and passes them to the Loko app on your phone over Bluetooth. One Ground can follow up to 30 trackers. It runs on an 18650 battery, which is not included.

To attach the tracker, you can print our free collar mount for collars up to 40 mm wide.

Know the limits before you head out:

  • If the tracker cannot get a satellite fix within 60 seconds, it sends its last known position. The app shows the time each position arrived but no warning that it is old, so check the time before you walk to a point.
  • The Ground stores positions while your phone is disconnected, but the app does not pull them in when you reconnect.
  • Loko holds no radio certifications. Check your local radio regulations before use.

FAQ

Questions Hunters Ask About Dog GPS Tracking

Can a dog GPS tracker work without cell service?

Yes, if it has its own radio link. Garmin Alpha, Dogtra PATHFINDER2, Dogtrace X30 and Loko all send the dog's position straight from the collar to a receiver you carry, with no SIM card or mobile network.

How far does Loko track hunting dogs?

20+ km in open areas with a clear line of sight. Trees, hills and valleys shorten the range, so test it in the ground where you hunt.

How long does the Loko battery last?

It depends on the update interval. Up to 1 year applies to long intervals. At the short intervals used for following a running dog, it lasts much less, and we have not yet published a measured runtime for those settings.

Can I use Loko on a dog that swims?

No. Loko is not waterproof and withstands light rain only. For water work, choose a collar with a published water rating.

Does Loko require a subscription?

No. Loko sends positions over LoRa radio to your own receiver, so there are no monthly fees.
