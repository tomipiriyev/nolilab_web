GPS Tracking for Farm Equipment: How to Choose, and Where Loko Fits

Short answer: pick a farm equipment tracker by two things: how it is powered, and whether there is mobile signal where the machine sits. Cellular and satellite trackers show a machine from anywhere and charge a plan. Loko shows parked implements, trailers and ATVs within radio range of a Loko Ground you keep on the farm, with no SIM and no plan. It is not an anti-theft system.

Updated 25 November 2026. Spytec and LandAirSea prices checked on the makers’ websites on that date. Prices change, so check again before you buy.

Two Kinds of “Farm GPS”

The GPS guidance domes and screens on a tractor steer it in the field. They are not trackers, and they are a common target for thieves. A tracker on the tractor doesn't protect them. Common rural crime advice is to remove guidance equipment and lock it away when the machine is parked, use PIN features where they exist, mark the units and record their serial numbers.

The rest of this guide is about trackers: devices that tell you where a machine is.

Choose by Power and by Signal

  • Power: a hardwired tracker runs from the machine's battery and can report often. A battery tracker needs no wiring and moves between machines, but reports less often to make the battery last.
  • Signal: a cellular tracker only reports where there is mobile coverage. A satellite tracker needs open sky. A local radio tracker like Loko needs the machine within range of your receiver.

Where Loko Fits on Machinery

  • Parked implements and trailers left around the farm, to see where they were left.
  • ATVs and utility vehicles that move around the property.
  • Many machines on one screen: one Ground follows up to 30 trackers, with no plan for each.

Where It Doesn't

  • Theft protection: see below.
  • Checking from home or town: beyond the Ground's radio range you see only the last position received.
  • Exposed to heavy rain: Loko is not waterproof (light rain only), so mount it under cover.

Loko Is Not an Anti-Theft System

Loko doesn't call or message you, and it only reaches you within radio range of your Ground. A machine driven off the farm soon drops out of range, and thieves can find and remove trackers. What Loko can do is show where a machine was last seen on your land, with the time the position arrived.

How Loko Works

The 14 g Loko Air gets its position from GPS and sends it over LoRa radio to the Loko Ground, which passes it to the Loko app on your phone by Bluetooth. If the Air can't get a fix within 60 seconds, it sends its last known position. When a tracker is out of range, the app keeps the last position with the time it arrived, but shows no warning that it is old. The Air keeps logging up to 3,100 positions, which you can read later over USB. You set the radio frequency for your region; see the LoRa frequency guide.

Battery life depends on the update interval: up to 12 months at long intervals, much less at short ones. For machines that sit parked for long periods, a long interval makes sense.

Test the Range on Your Own Land

  1. Mount an Air where it will really live on the machine, under cover.
  2. Drive or walk the farm with the Ground and note where updates stop, especially behind hills, in trees and near buildings.
  3. Find a high spot near the yard where the Ground sees more of the land.

What Loko Costs for 10 Machines

An illustration at list prices: a $119 kit plus nine extra Airs at $59 comes to $650, plus an 18650 battery for the Ground, with no plan over the years. Cellular and satellite trackers add a plan for each machine on top of the device, so compare their totals over the years you expect to use them.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do GPS trackers for farm equipment need a monthly subscription?

Cellular and satellite trackers do: they pay a network to send the position to you from anywhere. A local radio tracker like Loko has no plan, because it sends the position straight to a receiver you keep on the farm, but it only works within radio range of that receiver.

Will a GPS tracker stop tractor GPS theft?

No. The guidance domes and screens that thieves take are not trackers, and a tracker on the tractor doesn't protect them. Remove them and lock them away when the machine is parked, use the PIN features where they exist, mark them and record serial numbers.

Can Loko alert me when a machine is stolen?

No. Loko is not an anti-theft system. It doesn't call or message you, and it only reaches you within radio range of your Loko Ground, so a machine driven off the farm soon drops out of range.

How far does Loko reach on a farm?

20+ km in open areas with line of sight between the tracker and the Ground, and much less among trees, hills, sheds and buildings. Test it on your own land before you rely on it.

What does Loko cost for 10 machines?

$650: a $119 kit with one Air and one Ground, plus nine extra Airs at $59 each, with no plan after that. Add an 18650 battery for the Ground, which is not included. One Ground follows up to 30 trackers.
