Meshtastic vs a Dedicated LoRa GPS Tracker

Both use LoRa radio and work without cell service. They solve different problems.

Meshtastic is open-source firmware that turns LoRa boards into a mesh network for messages and shared positions. A dedicated point-to-point tracker such as Loko does one job: it sends a tracker’s position straight to a receiver you carry.

The short version: choose Meshtastic if you need people to message each other and can place relay nodes. Choose a point-to-point tracker if you need to find a small thing, such as a drone or a dog, and you want minimal setup.

How each one moves a position

Meshtastic uses managed flooding. A node that hears a packet lowers its hop limit and rebroadcasts it, unless it hears another node do so first. The default hop limit is 3 and the maximum is 7. A position can therefore travel around a hill if a node on that hill relays it.

A point-to-point tracker has one link. Loko Air sends directly to Loko Ground, which passes the position to the Loko app over Bluetooth. Loko has no relay or repeater function. If that one radio path is blocked, the position doesn’t arrive live, though the Air keeps logging to its onboard memory. Loko can also send over LoRaWAN, where a gateway you or a network provide receives the data. That is still not a mesh.

Meshtastic’s default channel uses a publicly known key, so anyone tracking privately should set up a channel with a random key. Loko’s peer-to-peer link uses AES-256 with a key you set.

Battery: what the role changes

Relaying costs power. A node that stays ready to rebroadcast has to keep listening. Meshtastic’s device roles reflect that. Its TRACKER role doesn’t retransmit routine traffic, and with power saving on it sleeps between position broadcasts. That means a Meshtastic tracker set up for battery life isn’t acting as a routine relay, so other nodes have to provide the mesh.

Comparing battery size alone won’t tell you runtime. Update interval matters more. As one example of Meshtastic hardware, the Seeed SenseCAP T1000-E has a 700 mAh battery. Loko Air has a 200 mAh cell and lasts about 2 hours at a 1 s interval, or up to 12 months at long intervals. Compare devices at the interval you’d really use, and see our battery life page for how Loko’s figures work.

Range: more hops is not the same as a better link

Both systems use LoRa, so a single hop follows the same physics. Terrain, antenna height and line of sight set the limit. Loko’s 20+ km figure applies in open areas with line of sight, and forests and hills shorten it. Our guide on how distance affects signal range explains why.

Mesh only helps when a relay is in the right place. In a remote valley with no other nodes, a mesh has nothing to relay through unless you bring and place a node yourself. In a town with active nodes, or on a hilltop you can reach, it can go much further than one link. Neither figure is worth much until you test it, so try a range test in your own terrain.

Which should you choose?

  • Choose Meshtastic if your group needs off-grid text messages, you want to place relay nodes, or you enjoy configuring radios. It’s also a low-cost way in: in November 2026, Seeed listed the T1000-E at $39.90.
  • Choose a point-to-point tracker if you’re recovering a drone, following a dog, or logging a route, and you’d rather have a small tracker and one receiver than a network to maintain.
  • Loko is the wrong choice if you need messaging, multi-hop relaying, or a device that survives being submerged. It isn’t waterproof and withstands light rain only.
  • Neither is right if you need a location from anywhere with no receiver nearby. Look at satellite and other options instead.

Check your local radio rules for either option. Permitted frequencies and transmit power vary by country.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Loko a Meshtastic device?

No. Loko sends over LoRa peer-to-peer or LoRaWAN. It can’t run Meshtastic, join a mesh or relay traffic.

Is Meshtastic the same as LoRaWAN?

No. Meshtastic is a mesh of peer nodes that relay for each other. LoRaWAN is a star network where end devices send to gateways connected to a network server.

Does a mesh give more range than a point-to-point tracker?

Only when relay nodes are placed where they help. Each hop is still a LoRa link limited by terrain and line of sight.

How many hops can Meshtastic make?

The default hop limit is 3 and the maximum is 7.

Loko Air sends its position directly to Loko Ground over LoRa, with no SIM, no subscription and no mesh to maintain.
