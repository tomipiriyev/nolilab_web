Offline GPS Tracker: What Offline Really Means

Four kinds of tracker work without internet on the tracker, but each one gets the location to you differently.

A GPS receiver works out its position from satellite signals alone, with no internet. The real question is how that position reaches you. A data logger keeps it until you collect the device. A local radio tracker sends it to a receiver you carry. A satellite tracker sends it up to a satellite network, and you view it online. A Bluetooth tag has no GPS at all: nearby phones report where they saw it.

Loko is a local radio tracker. The Loko Air tracker sends its GPS position over LoRa radio to a Loko Ground receiver, which passes it to the Loko app on your phone by Bluetooth. You don't need a SIM card, a subscription or mobile data.

The Four Kinds in Practice

GPS data logger

A logger records where it has been and keeps the track until you read it. The Columbus V-900 stores tracks on a microSD card. Its maker says a "SPY" mode, which wakes the logger at intervals to record and then sleeps again, runs for up to 30 days (measured at a 15-minute interval). A logger can't help you find something you have lost, because you only see the track once the device is back in your hand.

Local LoRa radio tracker

A LoRa tracker sends its position to a receiver you own. Nothing passes through a phone network or a cloud service, so it keeps working where there is no coverage of any kind. The limit is radio range: the tracker has to be within reach of your receiver. This is how Loko works.

Satellite tracker

A satellite tracker sends its position to a satellite network, so it can report from places with no phone coverage and no receiver nearby. You pay for a service plan, and the person viewing the location still needs an internet connection.

Bluetooth tag

A Bluetooth tag such as an AirTag has no GPS. Its location comes from passing Apple devices that detect it and report where they saw it. In towns that works well. On a remote trail with nobody around, the tag stops reporting. For a longer comparison, see AirTag vs LoRa tracker.

What Loko Does Out of Range

Loko's link is local: Loko Air → LoRa radio → Loko Ground → Bluetooth → the Loko app. You can expect 20+ km in open areas with line of sight. Hills, buildings, dense trees and a low antenna all cut that down. One Loko Ground can follow up to 30 trackers.

  • The tracker loses its GPS fix. After 60 seconds without a fix, Loko Air sends its last known position. The app does not warn you that the position is old, so a dot that has stopped moving may not be where the tracker is now.
  • The tracker goes out of radio range. Loko Air keeps logging its positions, up to 3,100 points.
  • Your phone disconnects from Loko Ground. The Ground keeps storing the positions it receives. When the phone reconnects, the app does not load those stored positions, so the gap does not fill in on the map.

Battery life depends on the update interval. Short intervals drain it much faster than long ones.

Where Loko Is the Wrong Choice

  • The tracker may end up far beyond your receiver. If it is driven away or carried out of range, no network picks it up. A satellite tracker covers that case.
  • Someone far away needs to follow you, or you need SOS messaging. Loko does not send your location across the world. That is a job for a satellite communicator; see how a local-radio alternative to Garmin inReach differs.
  • It will get wet. Loko is not waterproof and handles light rain only.
  • Everyday items in towns. For keys and bags where iPhones pass by all day, a $29 AirTag is simpler.
  • You only want a record of where you went. A data logger does that without a receiver.

FAQ

Can GPS work without internet?

Yes. A GPS receiver works out its position from satellite signals alone. Internet only matters for how the position is sent to you and displayed.

What does an offline GPS tracker mean?

It means the tracker doesn't use mobile data or Wi-Fi. A data logger stores the track until you collect it, a local radio tracker such as Loko sends it to your own receiver, and a satellite tracker sends it through a satellite network.

Can Loko show location if my phone has no signal?

Yes. Loko Air sends its position over LoRa radio to Loko Ground, which connects to your phone by Bluetooth. Neither needs mobile data.

How far does Loko reach?

20+ km in open areas with line of sight. Hills, buildings, dense trees and a low antenna reduce it.

What happens when Loko goes out of range?

Loko Air keeps logging up to 3,100 points. If it has no GPS fix for 60 seconds it sends its last known position, and the app does not warn you that the position is old.

Does Loko need a subscription?

No. There is no SIM card and no subscription. The Air + Ground bundle is $119, and an extra Loko Air is $59.

Is Loko a satellite tracker?

No. Loko is a local LoRa radio tracker. It does not send your location across the world and has no SOS messaging.
