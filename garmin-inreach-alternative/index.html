Garmin inReach Alternative: Satellite SOS or a Local LoRa Tracker?

Two different jobs: keeping a person reachable, or finding a dog, drone or gear nearby.

A Garmin inReach is a satellite communicator. It lets a person send SOS and messages from almost anywhere with a clear view of the sky, and it needs a paid plan. Loko is a local LoRa GPS tracker. It shows where a dog, drone or piece of gear is, within radio range of the receiver you carry, with no plan.

So the real question is not which one is better, but which job you need done. This page compares them with Garmin's own prices and says plainly where Loko fits and where it doesn't.

Updated 13 November 2026. Garmin, ACR, NOAA and Apple details checked on their own websites on that date. Prices change, so check again before you buy.

Loko is not an emergency device. It has no SOS button and no messaging, and it cannot contact rescue services. If someone's safety is the reason you are shopping, start with a satellite communicator or a personal locator beacon.

Cheaper Routes to Satellite SOS

  • Personal locator beacon (PLB): sends a distress alert through the Cospas-Sarsat system, with no subscription. The ACR ResQLink 400 lists at $409.95 to $459.95. In the United States, registering a beacon with NOAA is free and required by federal rules. A PLB does not send messages.
  • Your phone: Apple says Emergency SOS via satellite is free for two years after activating an iPhone 14 or later, in supported countries, and needs a clear view of the sky and horizon. Check your own phone and country.

Sources: ACR ResQLink 400, NOAA SARSAT FAQ, Apple Support.

Why an inReach Is a Costly Dog or Drone Tracker

  • Weight: over 100 g, against 14 g for a Loko Air.
  • A plan for that device: it needs an active plan to send anything.
  • Built for people: its strengths are SOS and messaging, which a dog or drone cannot use.

Loko's trade-off is range: it only reports while the tracker is within radio range of your Loko Ground, 20+ km in open areas with line of sight and much less among trees, hills and buildings.

Use Both

A common setup is an inReach or PLB on the person and a Loko Air on the dog, drone or pack, with the Loko Ground and phone in your pocket. The satellite device covers your safety; Loko shows where the tracked dog, drone or gear is while it stays within radio range.

What Loko Shows When the Tracker Is Out of Range

The app keeps the last position it received with the time it arrived, but shows no warning that it is old, so check the time. If the tracker cannot get a satellite fix within 60 seconds, it sends its last known position. Out of range, the Loko Air keeps logging up to 3,100 positions on the device, oldest overwritten first, which you can read later over USB with the configurator. The Ground stores positions while your phone is disconnected, but the app does not pull them in when you reconnect.

Know Loko's Limits

  • No SOS, no messaging, and no relay or mesh.
  • Within radio range of your Loko Ground only: 20+ km in open areas with line of sight, much less among trees, hills and buildings.
  • Not waterproof; light rain only. The Ground runs on an 18650 battery, which is not included.
  • Battery life depends on the update interval: up to 1 year at long intervals, much less at short ones.
  • Loko holds no certifications, so check your local radio rules for the frequency you set.

FAQ

Is Loko a Garmin inReach replacement?

No. Loko has no SOS and no messaging, and it cannot reach rescue services. It is a local LoRa GPS tracker for finding a dog, drone or gear within radio range.

Does Loko need a subscription like inReach?

No. Loko sends positions over LoRa radio straight to the Loko Ground and on to the app by Bluetooth, with no SIM and no satellite plan. An inReach needs an active Garmin plan.

What is the cheapest way to get satellite SOS?

A personal locator beacon has no subscription; in the United States, registering it with NOAA is free and required. Some phones also offer satellite SOS: Apple says Emergency SOS via satellite is free for two years after activating an iPhone 14 or later, in supported countries.

Should I put an inReach on my dog or drone?

It is a costly way to do it. An inReach weighs over 100 g and needs its own active plan, while a 14 g Loko Air sends to a Ground you carry with no plan. Loko only works within radio range, though.

Can I use both?

Yes. Many people carry an inReach or a beacon for their own safety and use Loko to keep track of a dog, drone or gear nearby. The two do not interfere with each other's job.

Loko finds your dog, drone or gear nearby with no SIM and no subscription. For your own safety, carry a satellite device too.
