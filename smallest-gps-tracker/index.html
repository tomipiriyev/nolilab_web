Mini GPS Trackers: How Small Can a Real GPS Tracker Be?

The smallest trackers compared by published size, weight, battery and how the location reaches you

Search for a "mini GPS tracker" and you will find devices from about 12 g to well over 30 g. They are not all doing the same job. Some have no GPS at all, some need a phone plan, and some only work within radio range of a receiver you carry.

This page compares small trackers using the makers' own published specs, explains why the smallest devices trade away battery or update rate, and says where Loko is the wrong choice.

Specs checked on the makers' websites on 5 November 2026. They change, so check again before you buy.

First Question: How Does the Location Reach You?

Size alone tells you little. What matters is how the tracker gets its position to your phone or screen:

  • Bluetooth tag (for example AirTag). No GPS chip. Nearby phones in the maker's network report where they saw the tag. Works well in towns, poorly where there are no people.
  • Cellular GPS tracker (for example Fi Mini, Tractive). Has GPS and a mobile modem. Works anywhere with mobile coverage, needs a paid plan, and the modem uses more battery.
  • Radio GPS tracker (for example Garmin dog collars, Loko). Has GPS and sends its position by radio to a receiver you carry. No plan and no coverage needed, but only within radio range of your receiver.

A GPS Chip Is Not a GPS Tracker

GPS modules themselves are tiny. A finished tracker also needs a battery, an antenna, a way to send the position (Bluetooth, a mobile modem or a radio) and a case. Those parts, especially the battery, set the final size and weight.

So when you compare small trackers, compare finished devices, and check what the listed weight includes. Tractive's CAT 6 Mini figure, for example, includes the collar.

The Size, Battery and Update Trade-Off

A smaller tracker has room for a smaller battery. To make that battery last, it has to wake up, get a fix and send less often. You can have any two of very small, long battery life and frequent updates, but rarely all three.

Loko shows this clearly. Its 200 mAh battery can last up to a year at long update intervals. At short intervals, the kind you would use to follow a moving dog or drone, it lasts much less. We have not yet published a measured runtime for those short settings.

Work Out Your Weight Budget

The number that matters is everything you attach, not just the tracker:

  • Tracker (Loko Air: 14 g)
  • Mount or case (for Loko, a printed collar mount and screws)
  • Fixings such as zip ties, tape or a harness

For an animal, ask your vet whether the total is suitable. For a drone, add the tracker and mount to the takeoff weight and check the rules where you fly.

Where Loko Is the Wrong Choice

  • You need to find it from far away. Loko only reports within radio range of your Loko Ground. If the item could end up in another town, a cellular tracker or a Bluetooth tag in a busy area is a better fit.
  • It will get wet. Loko is not waterproof. Do not use it on animals that swim or on anything left in heavy rain or water.
  • You want the lightest possible tag. An AirTag is lighter, but it has no GPS and needs other people's phones nearby.
  • You need frequent updates for weeks. At short intervals, the small battery will not last that long.

Loko fits when you will be within range, there is no mobile coverage or you do not want a plan, and you want real GPS positions in a 14 g package.

FAQ

Mini GPS Tracker FAQ

What is the smallest real GPS tracker?

It depends on what you count. AirTag (11.8 g) is smaller than most GPS trackers but has no GPS chip. Among trackers with GPS, Loko Air (14 g) and Fi Mini (about 16 g) are among the lightest we found, and they deliver the position in different ways: Loko by radio to your own receiver, Fi Mini over a cellular network.

How much does Loko Air weigh?

14 g, and it measures 30 × 22 mm. Add the mount and fixings to get the total weight you attach.

How long does a mini GPS tracker battery last?

It depends mostly on how often it reports. Small trackers carry small batteries, so frequent updates drain them quickly. Loko can last up to a year at long intervals and much less at short ones.

Does a mini GPS tracker need a subscription?

Cellular trackers such as Fi Mini and Tractive do. AirTag and radio trackers such as Loko do not.
